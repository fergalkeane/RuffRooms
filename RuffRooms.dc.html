Our story

We got tired of “dog friendly” meaning the car park

RuffRooms was started by Rachel and Fergal Keane, two people who kept booking places that said dogs were welcome and kept discovering, on arrival, that welcome came with conditions nobody had mentioned.

It started, as these things do, with one weekend that did not go to plan. The booking said dogs welcome. What it meant was dogs welcome in one ground-floor room, not past the lobby, not in the bar, not in the breakfast room, and there was a charge nobody had mentioned until check-in. Dinner was eaten in shifts, one of us in the car park.

It kept happening. A garden described as enclosed with a gate that did not latch. A size limit that appeared only once a Labrador walked through the door. A fee of fifty euro, discovered on the final bill. Every time, the information existed somewhere, in a policy document, on a phone call, in the head of the person on reception. It was simply never in the place where anyone was deciding where to stay.

What became obvious to Rachel and Fergal was that the problem was not unwelcoming properties. Plenty of Irish businesses are genuinely brilliant with dogs, and get no credit for it, because they sit in the same list as places that tolerate a dog through gritted teeth. Both get the same two words. Neither gets to explain.

So RuffRooms asks the questions instead. What is the charge, per dog, per night. How many dogs. What size, what breed, and are those rules real or theoretical. Is there somewhere safe to let a dog out. Can the two of you eat dinner in the same room. Every answer is confirmed with the property, published as a structured fact rather than a sentence in a footer, and re-confirmed every ninety days so it stays true.

We take no commission and hold no payments. Enquiries go straight to the property’s own inbox. That keeps us useful to owners rather than another cost, and it keeps us honest with dog owners, because we have nothing to sell them but an accurate answer.
